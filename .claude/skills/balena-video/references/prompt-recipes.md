# Anime generation and continuity

## Reference assignment
Attach the approved 2D Balena sheet or frame as character/style reference. Use an approved scene frame as the image-to-video start when supported. Keep the original product photography for the compositing stage. Capability names vary by provider: verify available reference, duration, aspect ratio and audio controls instead of inventing them. A prompt cannot enforce asset identity by itself.

## Reusable identity block
“Hand-drawn 2D anime animation with clean outlines, gentle cel shading and painted scenery. Balena matches the attached reference: a tiny, very round, wide blue whale with one combined head/body, cream belly with thin blue vertical stripes, small fins, short tail, big expressive eyes, pink cheeks and small smiling mouth. Brown coffee cup fixed on the head with the canonical blue splash behind it. Compact low whale posture throughout. Maintain identical silhouette, face, cup, splash and colors in every frame.”

## Christmas part 1, 8 seconds
Use the identity block. Bright ivory Christmas room with a beautifully decorated pine tree, cranberry ornaments, champagne-gold ribbon and warm fairy lights. A clear display area is reserved beside the tree for original product layers added later. 0–2 seconds: medium-wide view establishes Balena and the softly glowing tree. 2–6: Balena notices one light gently brightening and makes a small delighted fin gesture without rising upright. 6–8: camera eases toward the display area, Balena gazes toward it with a warm smile. Finish with stable framing for the next shot. Gentle animation, one main action, no rapid montage. No added text, logos, captions, speech, music or sound effects. No extra characters, limbs, accessories or generated packages. Negative prompt if supported: 3D render, photorealism, tall whale, human posture, feet, neck, fingers, stretched fins, distorted cup, flicker.

## Christmas part 2, 8 seconds
Start from the approved final frame of part 1, not an unrelated new reference. Same room, tree, lighting, scale and Balena design. 0–4 seconds: a gentle camera pullback reveals the reserved product display; Balena presents it with one small fin. 4–6: Balena blinks and smiles while fairy lights twinkle subtly. 6–8: settle into a still hero composition with clear space for the original package layers. Keep product placement assigned in the edit; do not hallucinate labels. No added text and fully silent. Match camera direction and lighting at the join.

## Eight-product alternative
When all eight SKUs must be shown, use multiple product cards or two groups of four with accurate original cutouts. Prefer simple cuts and short holds over complex moving shelves. For a single 8-second version, recommend fewer hero products or explain the readability limit. Do not silently drop products.

## Retry discipline
Generate one pilot shot first. Inspect silhouette, cup/splash, posture, brightness and temporal stability. Reject anatomical drift before buying more variations. Change one variable per retry; record model/settings, reference hash, prompt, cost and reason accepted/rejected. Evaluate cost per accepted clip, not just credits per generation. Never invent platform prices or equate credits across services.
